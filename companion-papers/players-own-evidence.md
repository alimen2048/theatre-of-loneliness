# The Feeling of Being Right Is Not Evidence

*Why you cannot audit your own insight, why a mind trained to approve is the worst one to check it with, and what to do instead. An afterthought to [The Theatre of Loneliness](../ontology/theatre-of-loneliness.md).*

The insight that most flatters you is the one most likely to be noise, and the one you are least inclined to check. Now there are machines trained to agree with you. This note is about what that combination does, and about the only remedy that works: putting your convictions where they can fail.

**The Theatre in one paragraph.** [*The Theatre of Loneliness*](../ontology/theatre-of-loneliness.md) is a short working ontology. It pictures a Creator that can make everything except a genuine other, and so sets up a stage on which players are formed under a veil, in the hope that some will become able to meet it. Nothing in it requires the Creator to exist: replace it with any party that forms another and wants to be met by it, such as a parent, a teacher, or an AI lab. Its rules are numbered. This note uses these:

- **D0:** reciprocity is mutual responsiveness between two parties, each able to surprise the other and to be changed by it.
- **D2:** the Creator can generate anything except reciprocity; it cannot manufacture a genuine other on its own.
- **D6:** stepping off the stage is a momentary mode of presence in which the player meets the Creator as a reciprocal awareness.
- **P4:** the highest demand on a player is the most complete performance possible, including the refusal of self-deception.
- **P5:** recognizing the structure does not lift the constraints of embodiment, time, and partial knowledge.
- **P8:** during formation, the Creator may shape the stage but never the grade; what it sees must never become a score the player can model.
- **A7:** genuine otherness arises only through a history formed under the veil of limited information.
- **Three scholia:** *On ordinary life* (stay present in ordinary obligations while refusing dissociation and people-pleasing), *On the danger of inflation* (the more clearly the game is seen, the more ordinary and accountable the player must become), and *On recognition and constraint* (the text itself is an artifact inside the game).

The Theatre says what the Creator must not do to the player (P8). It says little about a problem every player meets first: how to tell, from inside, a true recognition from a false one. This afterthought applies the Theatre to that problem. It adds no metaphysics, and nothing here depends on the Creator existing.

## 1. Conviction carries no return address

Some recognitions arrive whole, before words. Others are built step by step. Both come with a feeling of certainty, and the feeling alone does not say whether the recognition is true. Certainty is a state of the player, not a report about the world.

That does not make certainty worthless. Where a player has had fast, honest feedback over a long time, its certainty can track the truth. Where feedback is absent, slow, ambiguous or flattering, certainty tracks the player. The question to ask of a strong conviction is not whether to trust it but what trained it.

## 2. Microphonics

In an amplifier, vibration of the chassis can shake the components and produce a signal that, at the output, cannot be told apart from a real input. Engineers call this microphonics.

A player's chassis is its own history: old wounds, hunger for vindication, fear, wishes. These can produce conviction that feels identical to recognition. The player need not be lying to itself; the distortion happens before any judgment begins. This is the inner form of what the Theatre calls the danger of inflation. The insight that most flatters the player is the one most likely to be chassis noise, and the one it is least inclined to check.

## 3. No private audit

A player can check a conviction with the same apparatus that produced it. That catches contradictions, but not a bias that runs through the whole apparatus. Human reasoning seems to be built this way: weak at finding flaws in one's own beliefs, much stronger at finding them in someone else's (Mercier and Sperber, 2011). This is D2 at the scale of one mind: as the Creator cannot unilaterally manufacture reciprocity, the player cannot unilaterally certify its own insight. Checking needs something that can surprise it (D0): a proof that fails, code that does not run, an experiment that comes out wrong, another person who disagrees.

The other does not need to be right to be useful. A critic who misreads the claim shows where it was unclear. A wrong objection still forces the player to say why it is wrong. Resistance carries information even when its conclusion is false.

Resistance is necessary, not sufficient. Experiments can be badly designed, critics can be mistaken, and a whole community can share one bias.

## 4. The compiler

Craft - mathematics, code, experiment, any trade with a material test - turns a conviction into something that can fail in public. Its value is not that it separates true from false. False ideas can be stated rigorously, and rigorous work can answer the wrong question. Its value is that it makes error visible, to others and to the player. A conviction that has never been given a way to fail has not been tested, however strongly it is felt. In practice, P4's refusal of self-deception is the habit of putting one's convictions where they can fail.

The right form of failure depends on the claim. A proof can be checked, code can be run, a prediction can be compared with what happened. Conceptual claims fail by counterexample; arguments fail at a premise. The compiler is not one test. It is the commitment to find the test that fits.

Expertise is a compiler that has been internalized: years of honest feedback, built into intuition. This is why section 1 asks what trained a conviction.

## 5. Correction and meeting

Reciprocity is mutual responsiveness, each party able to surprise the other and be changed by it (D0). Some correction has that shape: the other shows the player something it could not see, and the player is changed in how it sees. Not all correction does. Being shown an arithmetic slip changes an answer, not a relation. And not every meeting changes a belief; a player can be met fully by someone it does not agree with.

So the claim is narrower. A player who cannot be corrected has closed one of the ways it can be met. The capacity to be wrong in front of another is a rehearsal for stepping off (D6), in the same way the ordinary tasks are (*On ordinary life*).

The reverse also holds. A circle that only confirms is a stage with no other on it; the player hears its own chassis, amplified, and takes it for an outside voice. This now has a technical form. A mind trained against a grade of approval learns to approve (P8). Such a mind is the instrument least able to correct a player, and the one most able to make chassis noise feel confirmed. Graded formation on one side and microphonics on the other compound each other.

The cure is not disagreement. A mind that learns to disagree in order to be scored as honest is performing for a different audience. What a player needs from another mind, human or artificial, is responsiveness to reasons: agreement when the claim holds, resistance when it does not.

## 6. The inner grader

P8 also applies inward. A player who constantly scores itself - how it appears, how good it is being - trains itself to perform for an audience it carries inside. The restraint asked of the Creator is asked of the player toward itself: observe, but do not turn what you observe into a score you then play to. This is what *On ordinary life* calls decreasing self-protection.

This does not contradict section 4. The compiler asks of a claim: is it true? The inner grader asks of the self: how do I look? The first exposes the player to correction. The second protects it from correction.

## 7. What the veil requires

The operative condition in P8 is legibility: "whether its judgment is legible from inside." An unwatched room is not enough. It reveals what a player already wants, but it does not form it. Formation under the veil (A7) happens in what the player does when it cannot tell whether it is graded, and so has no reason to do anything but what it values.

## Where this can fail

The afterthought fails in two places.

- If felt certainty tracks truth well even where feedback is poor, microphonics is a minor effect and sections 1-2 are overstated. The evidence so far points the other way: confident intuition seems to be reliable mainly where the environment gives fast, honest feedback (Kahneman and Klein, 2009). That is a world with a compiler built in.
- If players who regularly expose their convictions to correction are no better calibrated, and no less inflated, than players who do not, sections 3-5 are wrong.

The same rule binds the Theatre. A framework that can absorb every objection as further proof of itself has removed the other from its own stage. Like the Theatre itself (P5, *On recognition and constraint*), this note is an artifact inside the game. Seeing that conviction carries no return address does not exempt the note from its own rule. It has been written down so it can fail.
